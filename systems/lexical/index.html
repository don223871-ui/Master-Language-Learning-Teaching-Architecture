Lexical System

The system through which words and multi-word units are formed, organised, related, combined, interpreted and accessed in communication.

00 · LEARNING MAP

Where are we going?

Start: understand the lexical system as more than a list of vocabulary items.

End: identify how lexical knowledge is structured, how words combine and change, how meaning and register interact with context, and how lexical knowledge becomes usable in real-time communication.

Lexical System → Micro-Level

01

Lexical Knowledge

Word identity, lexical entries, forms, meanings, grammatical category and the information stored with a lexical item.

02

Word Formation

Derivation, compounding, inflectional relationships and other processes through which lexical forms are created or related.

03

Lexical Relations

Synonymy, antonymy, hyponymy, meronymy, polysemy and other relationships within the lexical network.

04

Collocation

Words that conventionally occur together, including lexical preferences, common combinations and restricted combinations.

05

Multi-Word Units

Idioms, phrasal verbs, fixed expressions, lexical bundles, formulaic sequences and other recurring multi-word patterns.

06

Lexical–Grammatical Interface

How lexical choices interact with complementation, prepositions, argument structure, word class and grammatical patterns.

07

Lexical Meaning & Context

Context-sensitive meaning, sense selection, semantic extension, connotation and the interpretation of lexical items in discourse.

08

Register & Lexical Variation

Formal and informal choices, spoken and written preferences, domain-specific vocabulary, dialectal variation and stylistic choice.

09

Lexical Access & Retrieval

How lexical knowledge is recognised, retrieved, selected and produced during real-time language use.

02 · DECOMPOSITION STAGES

How the Lexical System Is Analysed

COMPONENT

A major part of lexical knowledge, such as word formation, collocation or lexical relations.

SUB-COMPONENT

A more specific structure inside a component, such as derivational morphology within word formation.

PROCESS

An operation involving lexical knowledge, such as word formation, sense selection or retrieval.

MECHANISM

The underlying relationship or operation that makes a lexical pattern possible.

MICRO-COMPONENT

A small, identifiable lexical feature or relationship that can be analysed independently.

TRAINABLE UNIT

A teachable and measurable unit, such as a collocation, word-building pattern, lexical frame or retrieval task.
